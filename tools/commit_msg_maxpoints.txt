engine: `max_points` - the ceiling a per-point scaling needed, so 1506's speed trace can be written whole

「速度大于等于 160 时，使自身欢愉度提高 50%，之后每超过 1 点速度使自身欢愉度提高 2%，最多计入 100 点超出的速度。」

The engine already had `self_attr_above:<ATTRIBUTE>:<threshold>` - the EXCESS over a stated threshold, as a magnitude -
because a shipped clause needed it. What it had no way to say is the last part of this one: a CEILING on those points.
That left exactly two dishonest options, which is why the clause was registered rather than written: drop the bound
(280% at Speed 300 where the document says 200% - a silent wrong number) or approximate the bound.

`EffectSpec.maxPoints` states it, on the scale it belongs to. It bounds the POINTS rather than the resulting share,
which is the same thing here (the share is the points times a positive constant) and is what the document itself
states, so a reader can check the field against the sentence.

The clause is now written whole, as two effects that ADD: a flat 50%, and `self_attr_above:speed:160` at 2% per point
capped at 100 points. Measured through the judge: no Speed buff 0.1; Speed 174 (14 points over) 0.88; Speed 339 (179
over, capped to 100) 2.60 - where without the bound it would read 4.18.

Two findings from writing it, both measured:

  * the two effects must carry `coexist: true` on the scaled one. Two modifiers on the SAME attribute and target are
    judged the same kind and evict each other - isSameKind compares attribute, modifier type and source role, not the
    buff's name - so the trace read 0.38 (the per-point term alone) until it did;
  * the expectation has to be stated against the MEASURED base, not against zero: she carries 0.1 of her own Elation
    degree, which is what a first version of the judge got wrong.

Full suite: 726 suites, 2482 tests, 0 failures.
